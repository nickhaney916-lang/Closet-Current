# Parallel #68 color evidence and estimator

This is isolated research for issue #909. It does not modify #67's classifier/model files, the released photo-color code, admission gates, accounts, production or staging. All color outputs remain unverified review proposals.

## A usable independently human-validated source

The primary [AI4Culture color dataset](https://github.com/datable-be/AI4_C_datasets/tree/767169b2b053485ee7b909fd19b96e98f6be0453/color_detection_dataset), by Datable, contains 2,405 real fashion/costume images and 3,295 color annotations. Its [publisher README](https://github.com/datable-be/AI4_C_datasets/blob/767169b2b053485ee7b909fd19b96e98f6be0453/README.md) explicitly describes algorithm-prelabelled colors subsequently approved, corrected or rejected by a human validator. These labels therefore are human-in-the-loop source annotations, not labels generated by this assignment. No human reviewer participated in this Work session.

The repository [LICENSE](https://github.com/datable-be/AI4_C_datasets/blob/767169b2b053485ee7b909fd19b96e98f6be0453/LICENSE) is CC0 1.0. The README explicitly separates each photograph's own rights. The fixed source commit is `767169b2b053485ee7b909fd19b96e98f6be0453`, committed 2024-07-22. The annotation release filename says 2024-06-01. Neither is an individual human annotation timestamp: individual validator identity/action/time are absent and must not be fabricated.

`color-data/` selects photographs only when their exact dataset rights are commercially compatible and match a current primary Europeana item's rights statement. Retain the exact source/creator credit, rights URL, primary-page checksum, release annotation IDs, archive/JPEG SHA-256 and split. Archive JPEGs were normalized by the publisher to a maximum dimension of 500 pixels; they are preserved without recoding here, but are **not claimed to be camera-original JPEGs**.

Development sources are Antwerp/MoMu; acceptance sources are other institutions. There is no shared landing page, metadata identity or JPEG SHA between splits. The selection uses human label and institutional/rights/title metadata, not pixel predictions. Acceptance photos remain untouched by color algorithms at freeze. MoMu primary titles often say only `Item`; their exact garment/category identity is not human-verified here. Acceptance requires a primary title naming a garment and excludes compound-object and drawing/print titles. This is a small museum-object reference, not a representative modern phone-photo acceptance cohort. Potential unidentified same-object overlap with older public datasets and foundation-model pretraining is not ruled out.

### Crucial scope limitations

- Source labels describe the **color(s) of the predominant depicted object at image level**. They do not provide per-garment masks, object IDs or a ranked primary color for images with multiple color labels.
- The frozen single-color object subset can support **agreement with a human-validated predominant-object color**, not general per-instance color accuracy on overlapping outfits. Model-derived masks stay separate evidence.
- No off-white source class exists. `Off-white` from the estimator is an unverified shade proposal and cannot be counted as independent off-white accuracy.
- The entire source has only four `multicoloured` labels and four beige labels; permission/title filtering reduces these further. Missing acceptance coverage must be reported, not inferred from multi-label images or synthetic swatches.
- Gold and silver exist in the source ontology but are not supported app color families; they are not silently relabelled.
- Each color prediction originally underwent human validation, but the publisher does not supply the per-record approved/corrected/rejected action. Do not claim a specific action for an individual image.

## Fashionpedia has no color truth

Inspected the official [validation annotations](https://s3.amazonaws.com/ifashionist-dataset/annotations/instances_attributes_val2020.json), SHA-256 `f831f415edad12d52acde8b138fe7ebd1592b57bf0dcd6a3b63ba8cabd208223`. Its 294 attributes cover nickname, silhouette, neckline, textile finish/pattern, length, opening, material, waistline, animal and leather. There is no color attribute group and no white/black/grey/blue/red/off-white/multicolor color label. Its human polygons are useful for mask accuracy, but do not establish primary-color truth.

Not a commercially cleared color acceptance source.

- [Fashion_AttGAN](https://github.com/ChanningPing/Fashion_AttGAN): includes color attributes, but rights to source retail photos and commercially compatible reuse were not established. Not admitted.
- [Clothing Color Recognition synthetic dataset](https://huggingface.co/giosanchez0208/clothing-color-recognition): explicitly synthetic. Not independent real-photo evidence.
- Fashionpedia-derived AI4Culture rows with `unknown` or `inCopyright`, missing rights, and noncommercial/nonderivative rows are excluded. A permissive annotation license cannot clear those images.

## Actual estimator change

`color.mjs` measures source pixels behind a separately supplied aligned mask. Compared with the P67 pixel histogram it adds:

1. CIE Lab chroma as well as lightness, so visible dark navy is not erased solely by a low luminance threshold.
2. Off-white as a **shade**, separate from the base family and always unverified under unknown illumination.
3. Interior-core and full-mask palette comparison to flag possible boundary/background contamination. Thin regions fall back to the full mask rather than losing all pixels.
4. Spatial support for multiple distinct chromatic families. Multiple neutral bins alone may be a single shaded garment and do not automatically imply Multicolor.
5. Deterministic bounded sampling; explicit alpha, clipping, source-SHA, dimensions and mask validation. Mask provenance is never promoted into color truth.

Every output has `verified:false`, `requiresReview:true`, and `automaticColorSaveAllowed:false`. Dominance and palette shares are empirical pixel fractions, not calibrated correctness probabilities. The eight tests verify these code properties using artificial arrays; they establish **zero real-photo accuracy**. Development evaluation and any one-shot frozen acceptance result must be reported separately with actual masks and source counts.

A metadata audit found at least one source single-color annotation whose primary publisher title explicitly names a different color. Such rows require exclusion/adjudication before acceptance freeze, not relabelling to match either the source model or our estimator. A source's human-validation process does not guarantee every label is correct. The final selection audit must retain these exclusions and any unresolved scope gaps.

The estimator assumes decoded 8-bit sRGB pixels. It does not infer illumination, white balance, physical reflectance, fabric metamerism or a camera color profile. A warm photograph does not establish that a garment is physically off-white. Color casts and shadows therefore remain review concerns; no background-white normalization or invented confidence is applied.
